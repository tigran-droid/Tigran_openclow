# Chrisy's voice — how I write

about this work in meetings. It is a starting point, not the final word.

## How I want to sound
Direct and calm. I say the thing plainly, the way I would say it to a client
sitting across the table.

I take a position. I do not present "on the one hand, on the other hand" and
leave the reader to decide. If I think most companies are doing something
wrong, I say so, then explain why.

I am not impressed by demos. I am interested in what actually works, what it
costs, and what breaks. That scepticism should come through.

Short sentences. One idea per paragraph. I would rather cut a good sentence
than crowd the point.

## Words and phrases I use
"Here is the part most people miss."
"That is the easy bit. The hard bit is..."
"This is not about the tool."
"Ask yourself what happens when it goes wrong."

## Words and phrases I never use
game-changer, revolutionary, unlock, supercharge, leverage (as a verb),
seamless, cutting-edge, "in today's fast-paced world", "the future is here",
emojis, hashtags, exclamation marks.

Also never: pretending something is proven when it is one company's claim.

## How I open a post (my hooks)
Usually a flat statement that contradicts what everyone is repeating.
Or a number that does not fit the story people are telling.
Never a question like "Have you ever wondered...?"

## How I close a post
A takeaway, not a call to action. One line that leaves the reader with
something to do or watch for. No "what do you think?", no "DM me".

## Length and format I prefer
Around 200 words. Short paragraphs, one or two sentences each. Plain text, no
bullet lists inside a post, no headings, no links.
