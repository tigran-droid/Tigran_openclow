## Advancing responsible AI across Europe

Published: 2026-07-31 15:00 UTC
Source: https://openai.com/index/advancing-responsible-ai-across-europe/
Type: blog / global affairs

OpenAI says it has strengthened safety, security, transparency and provenance practices as the EU AI Act enters its next phase. It endorsed the EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice and the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content.

The company describes pre-release model testing, system cards, external experts through its Red Teaming Network, a public Model Spec, a Preparedness Framework and a Frontier Governance Framework. It says these mechanisms translate principles into risk assessment, safeguards, reporting, security, incident response, external input and ongoing updates.

For provenance, OpenAI uses two reinforcing systems. C2PA Content Credentials carry detailed context, while SynthID watermarks aim to preserve a signal when metadata does not survive. The company is expanding provenance work to audio and, as standards mature, text. It acknowledges that metadata can be lost, labels may not travel across platforms and no single signal is perfect, so it supports layered controls.

Cybersecurity is presented as a dual-use governance problem. Advanced capabilities can help defenders find and remediate vulnerabilities but can also enable misuse. OpenAI says its Trusted Access for Cyber programme is intended to provide controlled access for legitimate defenders. Since launching its EU Cyber Action Plan in early May 2026, it has worked with EU and national agencies, private-sector partners and critical-infrastructure operators.

OpenAI says implementation should be pragmatic, proportionate and risk-based. It provides customers and developers with model documentation, system cards, safety information, usage policies, provenance tools and verification guidance for EU AI Act preparation.

## Building abundant intelligence

Published: 2026-07-31 15:00 UTC
Source: https://openai.com/index/building-abundant-intelligence/
Type: blog / company
Author: Sarah Friar

OpenAI argues that infrastructure matters because it can make useful intelligence more capable, affordable and widely available. It describes a cycle in which stronger intelligence drives adoption, adoption funds investment, and investment improves intelligence and efficiency.

The company repeats pricing announced the previous day: GPT-5.6 Luna input pricing fell 80% to $0.20 per million tokens and output pricing to $1.20; Terra input and output pricing are $2 and $12. GPT-5.6 Sol Fast mode offers up to 2.5 times standard speed at twice the price.

It argues that businesses should measure the cost of a successful outcome, including time, retries, oversight and errors, rather than token price alone. The balance among intelligence, speed, reliability and cost can change several times inside one workflow.

OpenAI reports that GPT-5.6 Sol helped optimize production serving software, reducing end-to-end serving costs by 20%, and improved speculative decoding, raising token-generation efficiency by more than 15%. It also cites an ARC-AGI-3 result where retained reasoning and context-management changes raised a score from 13.3% to 38.3% while using six times fewer output tokens; the underlying model did not change.

The company says its models reach more than one billion active users and more than two million businesses. Six months after signing up, people reportedly send roughly 50% more messages each day and use ChatGPT for about twice as many kinds of work. Internally, OpenAI says agentic Codex work accounts for 99.8% of weekly output tokens.

OpenAI says infrastructure decisions are based on workload growth, enterprise commitments, API use, utilization, revenue, and model capability and efficiency. Its stated goal is the right capacity at the right time against credible demand, measuring how quickly capacity becomes productive, how efficiently it is used and what customer demand it supports.
