## Univé builds an AI-ready workforce

Published: 2026-07-31 07:00 UTC
Source: https://openai.com/index/unive/
Type: blog / customer case study

Univé, one of the Netherlands’ largest cooperative insurers, serves millions of members across insurance, mortgages, financial services and risk prevention. It treated AI as an organizational transformation rather than a technology deployment, aiming to build safe and responsible AI capability across its workforce.

Leadership brought the whole management community together for AI leadership sessions. Instead of concentrating on product demonstrations, leaders examined how work would change and how managers should create the conditions for responsible innovation. Director of Data and AI Yous van Halder said: “Most organisations try to scale AI by building more solutions. We chose to scale AI by creating more builders.”

Governance was designed into the rollout from the start. The controls include enterprise authentication, inherited connector permissions, privacy assessments, governance processes, security reviews, responsible AI principles, continuous monitoring and clear human accountability. Permissions follow the underlying enterprise systems, preventing AI access beyond what an employee is already authorized to see.

Employees receive permission, structure and dedicated time to redesign their own work. Collectively, they spend hundreds of hours each week building custom GPTs, experimenting with Workspace Agents and sharing successful approaches. ChatGPT Enterprise now supports work in claims, underwriting, finance, HR, legal, IT, customer service and management. Employees have created approximately 1,500 custom GPTs.

A pet-insurance claims agent assembles claim files, reviews veterinary invoices, checks policy conditions, finds missing information, highlights anomalies and prepares a traceable recommendation before the claims handler begins. Work that previously took hours to prepare can be ready for decision in minutes. The trained claims professional remains accountable for the final decision.

For underwriting, an agent reviews the incoming queue before the underwriter starts work. It combines approved enterprise information, identifies missing documents, flags risk indicators and highlights priority cases. The result is a prepared work queue with relevant context, supporting evidence and the areas where professional judgment is required.

Reported results: 97% of ChatGPT Enterprise licenses are activated; 85% of licensed users are active each week; active users average 40 prompts per week; employees have created around 1,500 custom GPTs; adoption spans virtually every knowledge-based function; and pet-insurance claim preparation has moved from hours to minutes.

The company’s lessons are to treat AI as an organizational capability, design governance into the rollout, invest in leaders as well as technology, give employees time and structure to experiment, and measure sustained adoption and employee capability rather than access alone. It is now exploring agents that proactively prepare recurring work across approved systems while keeping security, accountability and human judgment central.
