📢 la comunidad added that the campaign puts front and center people who have already lived through much of life, men and women who fell in love, broke up, made mistakes and started all over again.

🎥 The idea doesn't stop at traditional advertising. Influencers and creators are hitting the streets to invite older adults to share their perspective, while on social media people can submit their own questions and get answers from someone who has actually been there.

🥂 Rosario Garré, marketing manager at Finca Las Moras, put it simply: even as everything moves at an overwhelming pace, something magical still happens when two people sit face to face over a glass of wine, and that's the spirit the brand wanted to bring back to life.

Why it matters: for brands, this is a smart way to stand out in a market saturated with AI-generated or AI-assisted content, by leaning into something a model simply cannot replicate, lived experience. For the creative industry, it shows that human insight is becoming a marketing asset in its own right, not a step to automate away. And for young audiences scrolling for advice at 2am, it's a gentle nudge that outsourcing every emotional decision to a chatbot has limits.

Working every day between creativity and AI, I don't read this campaign as anti-technology, quite the opposite. AI is genuinely useful for organizing thoughts or finding the right words when you're stuck, but on something as human as love, the real difference is made by people who have actually felt heartbreak, made the wrong call, and tried again anyway. The most interesting future isn't AI versus human wisdom, it's figuring out how the two actually talk to each other.
