Ollama brings open models into Claude Desktop

Ollama just turned Claude Desktop into a multi-model app. With version 0.33, developers can flip one toggle and use open models like DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi or GLM right inside Anthropic's own interface, according to the company.

🔌 According to Ollama, setup takes just a few steps: open the Ollama app, select Claude, turn the integration on, and the third-party gateway configures itself automatically. No manual API keys, no config files to edit by hand.

🧠 Once connected, you can pick any model available in Ollama, whether it's running locally on your own machine or hosted on Ollama's cloud. Need a quick, private answer? Use a local model. Working on a larger coding or research task? Switch to a cloud model without ever leaving Claude Desktop.

🔒 On privacy, Ollama states that telemetry is disabled by default and that the company maintains what it calls a strict Zero Data Retention policy across both its cloud and local services. Anthropic's own polished desktop app effectively becomes a shell that can run on entirely different models underneath.

↩️ And if you want your original Claude setup back, the company says one toggle restores everything exactly as it was, no extra steps required.

Why this matters: for developers and creators, this collapses the wall between 'the app I like' and 'the model I can actually afford'. Instead of juggling separate tools for open-weight experimentation and production-grade tasks, you get one single interface that switches underneath you. For companies evaluating AI costs, it's a concrete way to test whether an open model can handle real workloads before committing to per-token pricing on frontier models, all without asking teams to learn a new tool.

What strikes me most about this move isn't really the engineering, it's the quiet shift it represents: interfaces are becoming more valuable than the models running behind them, and whoever controls the experience gains leverage even without owning the underlying intelligence. I find it more convincing when a company states plainly what it promises on privacy, rather than making triumphant claims about being unhackable or fully secure, and that kind of matter-of-fact transparency is something I'd like to see more often in this industry, especially as more players start stacking gateways on top of each other's products.

X launches MCP server to run ad campaigns with AI

X has just rolled out its new Ads MCP Server, a tool that lets advertisers build, refine and manage their X ad campaigns directly through AI assistants like Claude, ChatGPT or Grok, simply by typing a prompt instead of clicking through a dashboard.

🎯 According to X's own announcement, advertisers can now describe a campaign goal in plain language, for example a promotion with a fixed budget, a set timeframe and a specific target market, and let the connected AI tool handle the steps that used to require manually navigating the ads manager. One reported example prompt asked to 'launch a campaign for the fall drop... $15k over two weeks, US only.'

🤖 The Ads MCP connects any MCP compatible client, including Grok, Claude Code or a custom agent built on the MCP SDKs, to 23 dedicated advertising tools for reading campaign data, running analytics and managing ongoing ads. X says the goal is to work with whichever AI tool a team already prefers, rather than locking advertisers into its own native interface.

📈 This move puts X alongside Meta, TikTok, Pinterest and Snapchat, all of which have launched comparable MCP integrations for their ad platforms over the past year. It also builds on X's broader MCP push, after the hosted MCP server for its core API went live in June and the shift to pay per use API pricing earlier this year.

Why it matters: for marketers and agencies, this is a concrete step toward agentic campaign management. Routine tasks like campaign setup, budget tweaks and performance checks can move to a conversational layer, leaving more room for creative direction and strategic thinking. For X, it is another piece of Musk's everything app ambition, turning the platform into an operational hub that agencies and brands can plug their AI workflows into, not just a place to post content.

I find it telling that MCP integrations are becoming the default bridge between social platforms and AI agents this fast, almost every major network already has one. At the same time, I think the real challenge here is not technical but about trust: handing budget decisions and campaign edits to an AI agent means teams need clear guardrails, visibility into what actually changed, and an easy way to undo a mistake. Otherwise you end up automating the errors just as efficiently as you automate the boring parts of the job, and for an ad budget that is not a small detail.

Google unveils Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash for AI video

Google AI has just announced Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash, the newest version of its multimodal model built for video generation and editing. According to the company, it now carries over the creative controls people already love from Veo, plus a set of brand new capabilities.

🎬 Among the headline additions Google mentions are 4K upscaling, first and last frame control to precisely anchor how a clip starts and ends, and a fast 360p drafting mode designed to quickly test ideas before committing to a full resolution render.

⚡ What stands out is the direction Google has been pushing this model family in over the past months. The original Gemini Omni Flash launched at the end of June and was capped at 720p with a free upscale to 1080p. Moving toward native 4K signals a clear intent to compete head on with Veo and with the other high end video generation models currently on the market.

🧩 First and last frame control is probably the most practical addition for anyone doing structured storytelling. It lets creators lock the opening and closing of a scene instead of generating dozens of random variations and hoping one of them lines up with the rest.

🚀 The 360p fast drafting mode seems built for a different moment in the workflow: testing an idea quickly, adjusting the prompt, and only committing to the final high resolution render once the composition actually works.

Why it matters: for video creators, marketing teams and social media producers, this kind of granular control cuts down the trial and error that usually eats up production time. For companies building AI video into their pipelines, being able to draft fast at low resolution before committing to a 4K render means saving on compute and likely on API credits, without giving up quality when it actually counts to ship the final asset.

My take: what convinces me more than any single feature here is the 'fast draft, then final quality' logic behind this update, because that is exactly how real creative work happens. You test, you discard, you adjust, and only at the end do you spend real time and resources on the definitive version. If Google can genuinely bring that workflow into a generative video model, it changes how we think about AI assisted production, not just the resolution of the final output.

Claude now browses the web on its own, inside its own browser

Anthropic just gave Claude something it never had before: a browser built right into Cowork. No extension to install, no setup, it just opens when a task needs it.

🌐 Claude now has its own browser inside the Cowork desktop app. When a task needs to use a website, a browser opens in the side panel and Claude navigates webpages, reads them, clicks, and types, finishing the job while you keep working on something else.

🧩 What makes this different from Claude in Chrome, the extension Anthropic already shipped, is isolation. That older setup lets Claude act inside your actual browser, using your open tabs and existing logins. The new built-in browser is separate: nothing shared from your own browser unless you choose to, and it runs no extension at all.

🚀 The rollout is already moving. It's rolling out this week to Pro, Max, and Team plans in the Claude desktop app on macOS, Windows, and Linux (in beta), and Enterprise admins can turn it on for their organization starting today. If you already use Claude in Chrome, that stays your default, otherwise Claude switches to the built-in browser automatically.

⚠️ Anthropic isn't hiding the tradeoffs either. The company states plainly that the built-in browser carries the same prompt injection risks as any AI agent that acts in a browser, where instructions hidden in a page try to redirect Claude. It says the same safeguards used for Claude in Chrome apply here, but admits those measures meaningfully reduce the risk without eliminating it, and recommends starting on sites you actually trust.

Why it matters: for anyone dealing with internal dashboards, vendor portals, or legacy systems that have no direct integration, this means handing off the tedious browser part of a task without connecting Claude to your personal browser and its logins. For teams and companies, it's a more contained way to let an AI agent act online, kept separate from your own tabs and accounts by default.

What convinces me here isn't the browsing itself, agents doing web tasks is old news by now, it's the decision to keep this browser isolated from your own instead of just plugging Claude into everything you already have open. That's a deliberate tradeoff between usefulness and control, and the fact that Anthropic talks openly about the security limits instead of selling this as a finished, risk-free feature is exactly what makes me trust these tools a little more when I recommend them to the people I work with.
