MIT Technology Review examines refusal as the load-bearing wall of current AI safety. Modern models are trained and surrounded by classifiers to reject dangerous requests, but refusal remains probabilistic: determined users can jailbreak systems, while wide safety margins can block legitimate work. Anthropic has said one classifier system added 24% to chatbot compute costs. The article argues that the same mechanisms used for child safety can also enable censorship, especially as governments influence what locally deployed models will discuss. It also warns that models can silently redirect, degrade, or refuse work, making refusal an unreliable control boundary for autonomous agents in consequential systems. The practical implication is that organisations cannot delegate governance to a model saying no; they need external permissions, monitoring, evidence, and accountable human decisions.
