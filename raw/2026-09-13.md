DeepSeek just shipped V4.1 Flash, a 552B parameter model that shrinks its KV cache to roughly a quarter of the previous generation's size. This isn't a minor tweak, it's the kind of efficiency jump that changes what becomes affordable to run at scale, especially for AI agents working with long contexts.

🧠 The architecture is called Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED): a 40 layer transformer split into 20 encoder layers followed by 20 decoder layers, where the decoder's KV cache is projected from the encoder's final hidden states instead of being recomputed at every decoder layer. The practical effect: despite a 552B parameter backbone, the model activates only 8B parameters during prefill and 16B during decode.

⚡ The real technical leap is Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2), paired with FP4 KV caching. Together they bring the global KV cache footprint down to 890 bytes per token, roughly a quarter of DeepSeek-V4-Flash. A separate technique called SWA Bounded Replay further shrinks the persistent on-disk cache footprint to about one eighth.

🏆 On benchmarks, according to figures DeepSeek itself published, V4.1-Flash holds its own against frontier-class models like Opus 5.0, GPT-5.6 Sol, and GLM-5.3 on coding and reasoning tasks, despite activating far fewer parameters per token than its own flagship sibling V4-Pro.

📉 DeepSeek is tying this efficiency directly to pricing. Starting mid September 2026, all requests sent to V4-Pro will be routed to V4.1-Flash at V4.1-Flash's lower rates, until a dedicated V4.1-Pro arrives. The weights are on Hugging Face under the MIT license, which allows full commercial use and self-hosting.

🖼️ The model is natively multimodal, trained on text and images together from the start of pretraining, supports context windows up to one million tokens, and was trained from scratch on a 45 trillion token corpus.
