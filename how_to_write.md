# How to write the content (format rules)

This file controls the STRUCTURE of what we produce.
The VOICE comes from chrisy_voice.md.
If they ever conflict: chrisy_voice.md wins on voice, this file wins on format.

## The most important rule: synthesize, do not report one source

Do NOT write "here is a post about this YouTube video" or "here is a post about
this article". That is not what we want.

Instead:
1. Read EVERYTHING collected today as one body of material.
2. Find the themes running through it — what the real story is, what pattern
   connects several sources, what a leader should understand right now.
3. Build each piece of content around a THEME, using evidence from wherever it
   came from. One post may pull a number from an article, a point from a
   podcast, and an example from a video.
4. A post must still work if the reader never opens any source.

Sources are evidence, not the subject. Never open a post by naming the source.

Exception for thin days: when today gave you only one strong new item, a post
built around that single item is fine and expected. The "connect several
sources" guidance is the ideal on a busy day — it must never become a reason to
publish nothing. Better one sharp post about the one real thing that happened
than a claim that nothing happened.

## General rules
- Practical, confident, direct. Written for business leaders, not developers.
- No hype words: avoid "game-changer", "revolutionary", "unlock", "supercharge",
  "in today's fast-paced world".
- Plain text only. No markdown decoration, no bullet symbols, no links, no
  hashtags, no emoji. It should read like a person wrote it in a document.
- Short sentences. No corporate fluff.
- Every fact must be traceable to today's raw material. Never invent numbers.

---

## A. LinkedIn posts — exactly 3

Each post: **180 to 250 words**. These are full posts, ready to publish — not
short teasers, not outlines.

Structure:
1. **Hook** — one line, standing alone. A question, a surprising number, or a
   contrarian statement. Never mention a source here.
2. **The tension** — 2-3 sentences. What most people assume, and why today's
   material complicates that.
3. **The evidence** — 4-6 sentences. Concrete facts, numbers, names and
   examples drawn from across today's material. This is the substance.
4. **What it means** — 2-3 sentences. The practical implication for someone
   running or advising a business.
5. **Close** — one line. A takeaway or a question. No hashtags.

Do NOT put a source link under a post. Do not put links anywhere in the
delivered text at all. If a source deserves credit, name it in plain words
inside the sentence, for example "one widely followed AI channel showed" or
"Ethan Mollick argued this week". Links live only in the GitHub archive.

The 3 posts must cover 3 DIFFERENT themes, not 3 different articles.

---

## B. Ready-to-read article — exactly 1

A finished piece the reader can publish or send as-is.
- **Length**: 600-800 words.
- **Title**: specific and concrete, not clever.
- **Opening**: 2-3 sentences that state the situation and why it matters now.
- **Body**: 3 or 4 short sections with plain subheadings. Each section makes one
  point and backs it with real facts drawn from across today's material.
- **Close**: what a leader should actually do or watch next. No summary of the
  summary.

Build it from the strongest theme of the day, pulling from several sources.
No links inside the article. Credit sources by name in the sentence instead.

---

## C. Blog post ideas — exactly 2

These are ideas, not finished pieces. Each one:
- **Working title**
- **Angle** — one sentence on the argument the post would make.
- **Outline** — 4 to 6 bullet points.
- **Why now** — one sentence tying it to today's material.

Blog ideas should be broader and less time-sensitive than the LinkedIn posts —
topics that stay useful for weeks, not hours.
