# How to write the content (format rules)

This file controls the STRUCTURE of what we produce.
The VOICE comes from chrisy_voice.md. The AUDIENCE comes from client_profile.md.
If they ever conflict: chrisy_voice.md wins on voice, this file wins on format.

## General rules
- Practical, confident, direct. Written for business leaders, not developers.
- No hype words: avoid "game-changer", "revolutionary", "unlock", "supercharge",
  "in today's fast-paced world".
- Short sentences. No corporate fluff.
- Every claim must be traceable to something in today's raw material.

---

## A. LinkedIn post ideas — exactly 3

Each one:
1. **Hook** — one sharp line that stops the scroll. A question, a surprising
   number, or a contrarian take.
2. **Body** — 3-4 sentences. Explain the news in plain terms, then say what it
   actually means for a business leader.
3. **Source** — one link to the original.

Each post must come from a different underlying story. Number them 1, 2, 3.

---

## B. Ready-to-read article — exactly 1

A finished piece the reader can publish or send as-is.
- **Length**: 500-700 words.
- **Title**: specific and concrete, not clever.
- **Opening**: 2-3 sentences that state the situation and why it matters now.
- **Body**: 3 or 4 short sections with plain subheadings. Each section makes one
  point and backs it with a real fact, number or quote from today's material.
- **Close**: what a leader should actually do or watch next. No summary of the
  summary.
- **Sources**: list every link used at the bottom.

Build it from the strongest theme of the day. If several sources cover the same
story, combine them into one deeper article rather than repeating.

---

## C. Blog post ideas — exactly 2

These are ideas, not finished pieces. Each one:
- **Working title**
- **Angle** — one sentence on the argument the post would make.
- **Outline** — 3 to 5 bullet points.
- **Why now** — one sentence tying it to something in today's material.
- **Source links**

Blog ideas should be broader and less time-sensitive than the LinkedIn posts —
topics that stay useful for weeks, not hours.
