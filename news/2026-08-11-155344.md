## The one-person AI consultancy
Nate Herk argued that a solo operator can now deliver internal AI workflows with natural-language development tools.
His commercial sequence is education, audit, project, then retainer.
The practical advice is to build a workflow for your own business first and use real client conversations to discover the work worth changing.

## An AI SaaS built in public
Futurepedia demonstrated a product journey from research and positioning through authentication, subscriptions, onboarding and security review.
The example considered a $39 subscription against roughly $3 in maximum monthly inference cost per user.
The presenter then warned that support, billing failures, maintenance and churn remain after the code is generated.

## From loops to agent graphs
The AI Daily Brief described graph engineering as the design of relationships between agents, tools, knowledge sources and humans.
A loop governs how one agent repeats work towards a finish line.
A graph defines specialisation, handoffs, permitted data flows and failure routes across a larger system.
The business implication is organizational: more agents require explicit ownership, not merely more automation.
