# Daily news — 2026-08-01

## AI enters the business model
Enterprise conversations have moved from whether agents matter to how they change products and pricing.
Examples included outcomes-based fees instead of hourly billing and audits becoming persistent rather than occasional.
The practical question is shifting from “where can we save time?” to “what should we now sell differently?”
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qUXAk4iZs4k

## LinkedIn adds an AI-slop signal
LinkedIn has added a “seems like AI slop” feedback option, according to a current product demonstration.
It gives readers a way to flag content that feels mass-produced and low quality.
For companies, cheap content generation now carries a visible reputational risk.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sUmx-Yi6TwE

## Robots handle delicate tasks
Google demonstrated Gemini Robotics ER2 placing grapes into a zip bag, closing it, unscrewing a light bulb and tying a rubbish bag.
These are vendor demonstrations, not proof of production reliability.
But they show physical AI moving towards tasks requiring dexterity, pressure control and multi-step manipulation.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sUmx-Yi6TwE

## Agents move into shared workrooms
A shared collaboration product demonstrated several coding agents building websites, reviewing one another and discussing revisions in the same environment.
The first outputs varied substantially in quality.
The useful shift is not more chatbots; it is visible coordination between creators, reviewers and humans.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sUmx-Yi6TwE

## Frontier access moves behind the interface
Kimi paused new paid subscriptions after heavy demand, while its developer playground still offered access after a small credit payment.
The demonstration showed a one-million-token context setting and adjustable reasoning controls.
Model availability increasingly depends on which official access layer a team understands.
