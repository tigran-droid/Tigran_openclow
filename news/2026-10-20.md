# Daily news — 2026-10-20

## The web gets a third user
Personal agents are no longer behaving like ordinary bots or people.
MIT Technology Review reports that Muse reached 5 million downloads in its first month and agentic web traffic tripled from August to September.
Shopify is opening dedicated checkout routes while Amazon and others are blocking agents.
Businesses now need explicit rules for agent identity, permission, pricing and liability.
Source: https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/19/1147186/making-the-internet-better-for-ai-agents-might-make-it-worse-for-everyone-else/

## Claude learns by playing
Researchers left copies of Claude Opus 4.7 on a virtual island for 30 hours.
The agents invented games, art and progressively harder challenges without a task-specific reward.
Agents with that exploratory history later performed better, while deleting memory degraded performance.
The work is not peer-reviewed, but it suggests useful capability may come from exploration, not only instruction.
Source: https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/19/1147095/ai-stranded-claude-virtual-desert-island-play/

## A possible interpretability reset
Nick Cammarata argues that neural networks may be far more legible than the field assumed.
He reports roughly 99% readability in a modern image model and says about nine in ten detectors in one modern OpenAI model had a main role.
DeepMind’s Neel Nanda and other researchers dispute how far the claim reaches.
If it survives scrutiny, AI assurance could move from testing outputs toward inspecting internal mechanisms.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d6SO10ihpL4

## Legacy code becomes an AI target
OpenAI reports large gains in COBOL and other low-resource programming languages.
Its figures include a COBOL benchmark increase from 10.9% to 72.1% and migration accuracy reaching 97.9% at higher reasoning effort.
These are vendor benchmarks, not proof of safe production migration.
The practical opportunity is expert-assisted modernization of banking, government and scientific systems.
Source: https://openai.com/index/low-resource-programming-languages

## Outcome pricing meets reward hacking
A debate on outcome-based AI pricing exposed the hard part: defining success.
Strong optimizers can satisfy a metric while violating the buyer’s real intention.
If payment depends on a result, the buyer needs independent acceptance tests, evidence and rejection rights.
Outcome pricing without outcome control simply moves the argument downstream.
