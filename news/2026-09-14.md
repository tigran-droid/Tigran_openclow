# Daily news — 2026-09-14

▸ Frontier labs move towards safety cases
Sam Altman said OpenAI now prepares explicit safety cases before reinforcement-learning runs expected to produce significant capability gains.
He also backed consistent frontier rules, independent auditors and shared standards for misalignment and monitoring.
For businesses, safety evidence is moving closer to the development process rather than arriving as a policy document after release.

▸ Anthropic engineers favour reusable skills
A detailed walkthrough of Anthropic's approach argued against rebuilding a separate agent for every job.
The emerging stack is a general agent plus task-specific skills containing process, context, scripts and examples.
The practical lesson is to preserve proven work and verify outputs before a human sees them.

▸ Current models are already economically disruptive
Ethan Mollick argued that current frontier models can perform weeks of human work when properly guided and harnessed.
He warned that pacing future models will not remove the need to prepare for job and organisational change now.
Transformation plans need an AI-human work model, not a promise to wait for the technology to settle.

▸ Data sovereignty meets maintenance reality
Mollick also challenged the assumption that small fine-tuned models are an easy route to control.
Keeping them ahead of rapidly improving general models can be expensive and difficult.
Buyers need to price continuous evaluation and maintenance, not just hosting and initial fine-tuning.
