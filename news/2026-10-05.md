# Daily news — 2026-10-05

## Team email needs an agent-native redesign
Ethan Mollick argued that teams need an email system where humans and autonomous agents can work together.
That means shared access, explicit permissions, comments and escalation to a human.
The opportunity is a governed workspace where responsibility remains visible.
Source: https://x.com/emollick/status/2106995773682458784

## Personal computers need an audit layer
Mollick called for personal operating systems designed for AI co-working.
Requirements include fine-grained security, visible agent activity and independent audit agents.
Agent governance is becoming a product requirement.
Source: https://x.com/emollick/status/2106936117421601110

## The AI skill bottleneck is moving
Mollick argued that AI is becoming easier to use, making individual prompting skill less durable.
He sees organisational systems increasingly limiting gains, while whole job categories may be affected.
Leaders should invest in workflow ownership and operating redesign.
Source: https://x.com/emollick/status/2106914993526747224

## One prompt now produces a working game
A Fable 5.1 demonstration generated a detailed brutalist city builder from one prompt.
It is not proof of production reliability, but it shows the falling cost of a functional first version.
Companies can shorten prototype cycles while preserving acceptance and security tests.
Source: https://x.com/emollick/status/2106768373736493399

## Formal verification concentrates human judgment
Alex Wissner-Gross predicted that, if P versus NP is solvable, AI could solve it within two to three years.
He described Lean's value as concentrating human verification on a small, heavily audited kernel.
Business AI can apply the same control principle.
