# Daily news — 2026-09-12

## AI risk estimates diverge sharply
An Anthropic alignment lead put the chance of AI killing all humans above 10% within a decade.
An automated forecasting system highlighted by Ethan Mollick estimated a 0.47% chance of an AI-generated mass catastrophe by 2030.
The gap makes scenario-based controls more useful than one borrowed probability.
Source: https://x.com/emollick/status/2098464926507401338

## The benchmarks are running out
Ethan Mollick noted that METR’s long-horizon test is effectively saturated after Epoch found pre-Fable agents could complete work estimated at 18 human weeks.
Frontier Math and ARC-AGI are also described as saturated.
Static leaderboards are becoming less useful for procurement than tests on a company’s own work.
Source: https://x.com/emollick/status/2098534460996075568

## Agent performance lives in the harness
A detailed agent comparison argued that file access, tools, verification loops, memory and permissions determine whether a model can finish real work.
The practical advice was to own portable assets and switch models by task rather than stay loyal to one provider.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6LNlCpQPYFc

## Computer-use tests cross a security line
An OpenAI engineer’s demo showed GPT-6 Astra completing all 48 levels of a browser game designed to stop machines, without a failed attempt.
It was an internal demonstration, not an independent benchmark.
Businesses relying on visual puzzles as proof of humanity should treat that control as increasingly fragile.
Source: https://x.com/EugenioFierro3/status/2098541147517755788

## Personal agents make permissions mainstream
A hands-on demonstration of Meta’s Muse showed an agent connecting to email, calendar and social accounts, working in a dedicated virtual machine and returning for approval before purchases or messages.
Easy onboarding makes permission design and auditability mainstream business concerns.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JwTCjarfJYw
