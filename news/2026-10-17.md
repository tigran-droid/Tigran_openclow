# Daily news — 2026-10-17

## Agent teams coordinate without a manager
Ethan Mollick highlighted a Gemini 4 experiment in Minecraft.
Multiple agents developed spontaneous coordination to complete a complicated goal.
The useful question for companies is no longer whether one agent can do a task.
It is whether the work, handoffs and failure conditions can be designed for a team of them.

## The valuable skill is context extraction
Matt Pocock argued that better models do not remove the learning curve.
Agents still need people to explain what they care about and surface missing assumptions.
His practical method is to have the agent interview him relentlessly before building.
For transformation work, eliciting requirements is becoming more valuable, not less.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0

## Software can be rebuilt faster than its moat
One developer reportedly used Claude to reconstruct much of Adobe's software in a clean-room project.
The result was incomplete and buggy, but the story was enough to put pressure on Adobe's share price.
Code is becoming cheaper to reproduce.
Support, integration, trust and ownership of the workflow become more important as a result.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xIFMwhvvUUo

## Creative production becomes direction
A reported AI music video was produced through 21 messages in 19 hours.
Claude Code coordinated research, lyrics, storyboards, code, editing and sound while specialist tools generated the assets.
The scarce skill was not operating each application by hand.
It was holding taste and continuity across the whole production.
Source: https://x.com/EugenioFierro3/status/2111212129856389457

## Browser agents cross the action boundary
A new ChatGPT Chrome workflow was reported as able to read across tabs and act inside logged-in sessions.
That can remove repetitive copying, form filling and navigation.
It also turns browser access into operational authority.
Permission design and a visible action trail now matter as much as convenience.
