## Custom software reaches the “why not?” price
Andrej Karpathy gave Opus 5 the opening of The Lord of the Rings and a one-million-token budget.
For roughly $10 and two hours of work, it produced 5,500 lines of code for a procedural 3D rendering.
The result was imperfect, but it demonstrated a new category of work: deeply bespoke software that was previously too expensive to justify.

## AI automation pricing gets concrete
One practitioner shared lessons from selling more than 100 AI automation systems.
His example priced a lead-booking agent at $5,500 against $41,600 in estimated annual labour savings, plus $400 a month for maintenance.
The important lesson was to measure the baseline and the result after one, two and three months, because an unmeasured automation is difficult to defend or expand.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lg5TYWPSg6M

## Voice becomes a building interface
A current demonstration used voice instructions to create and repeatedly modify an interactive 3D model.
The user changed colours, proportions and components without working through a conventional design interface.
This lowers the barrier to prototyping, but the demonstration did not establish production reliability, governance or quality.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaNIJ9l2NOE

## Consistent AI video becomes the target
xAI says Grok Imagine Video 1.5 can use image and voice references, generate native 1080p video and hold selected elements consistent across scenes.
The company says users can provide up to seven references per generation.
If the claims survive real production tests, the business value is less about generating clips and more about reducing the repeated work required to preserve brand, character and product consistency.
