# Daily news — 2026-10-26

## Manufacturing's immediate AI prize is paperwork
A business discussion argued that planning, transactions, documents and scheduling offer more immediate value than factory robots.
The speaker estimated a 10 to 30 percent improvement to the bottom line.
The estimate is not a measured result, but it points toward practical workflow automation available now.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iO8WdmgNB2E

## AI development starts to close its own loop
A researcher described AI-written code as a weak form of recursive self-improvement.
His team wants humans to set rewards, environments and goals while machines handle ideation, implementation and validation.
This makes independent tests and well-designed objectives more valuable.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=48AVDDogtVw

## Always-on agents move toward the operating layer
A discussion of OpenAI's Dots described cloud-based agents connected to more than 4,000 applications and working against persistent goals.
The strategic claim is that the relationship and context around an agent become more valuable as models become less differentiated.
Companies should own permissions, context and acceptance rules.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Ye-VuYMSAA

## AI may widen senior management's span
Ethan Mollick observed that AI lets senior leaders directly monitor more of an organisation.
He described this as easier to implement than a full redesign.
Visibility may remove reporting friction, but it can also centralise decisions away from local context.
Source: https://x.com/emollick/status/2114337256865493250

## Raise ambition until the model breaks
Mollick advised users to keep making an AI task more ambitious.
Success reveals capability; failure maps the frontier.
That is a stronger capability test than repeating safe tasks the organisation already knows AI can do.
Source: https://x.com/emollick/status/2114503849826549842
