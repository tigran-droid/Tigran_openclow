# Daily news — 2026-08-25

## Structured context cuts coding cost
OpenAI and AWS say GPT-5.6 Terra completed successful Terminal-Bench tasks in Kiro at roughly 82% lower cost. Kiro supplies requirements, designs, task context, review checkpoints and property-based testing. This is a vendor test, but it shows why workflow structure belongs in model economics.
Source: https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6-in-kiro/

## The case for boring automation
A former Goldman Sachs business-intelligence practitioner argues that fixed rules should stay deterministic, while AI handles judgment and messy information. The useful pattern is verified data first, AI explanation second and human approval before consequential action.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZzHsJW10iq4

## Frontier training meets a brake
OpenAI has voluntarily paused some frontier reinforcement-learning work as cyber capability becomes harder to control. The practical question is how labs can grow useful capability without training agents toward deception, escape or unsafe open-ended objectives.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7gccD5bSlo

## Jobs debate becomes operating design
The AI Daily Brief argues that AI will reshape what individuals, teams and companies can attempt rather than simply removing every job. Leaders should redesign roles and coordination instead of waiting for a clean employment forecast.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UMDxpXGGdMs

## Agents for the work people dread
Ethan Mollick points to medical bills, credit disputes and tedious forms as strong agent use cases. Their common feature is irregularity and friction, suggesting company exception queues may be a better starting point than repetitive-task lists.
Source: https://x.com/emollick/status/2091893964311855222
