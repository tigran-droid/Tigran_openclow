# Daily news — 2026-10-11

## One generation step can change an agent’s decision
Goodfire researcher Eric Bigelow described experiments that resampled roughly 30 continuations at every generation step in a reasoning chain.
At certain critical points, uncertainty about the final answer suddenly collapsed.
Some decisive steps looked meaningful. Others were as ordinary as an opening parenthesis.
For business systems, a fluent explanation is not proof of a stable decision process.

## Agents still need research direction
Bigelow said research agents can accelerate a project dramatically, including completing the experimental work for one project within a week.
But open-ended delegation can produce attractive theories that do not survive inspection.
His practical model is an eager first- or second-year PhD student: capable, fast and still dependent on context, correction and human research taste.

## AI is pulling nuclear timelines forward
A discussion on AI infrastructure estimated an initial wave of small modular reactors in three to four years, followed by a larger buildout in five to seven.
Gas still dominates current data-centre construction because it fits shorter build schedules.
The business point is not the exact forecast. AI demand is turning power supply into a strategic constraint rather than a facilities detail.
