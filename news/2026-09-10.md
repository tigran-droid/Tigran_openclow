▸ Astra moves deeper into ordinary work
OpenAI says GPT-6 Astra can write code and operate desktop applications even without APIs.
Pricing starts at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens.
Vendor-reported tests show stronger document work, fewer retries and better long-running agent performance.
The practical question is whether those gains survive a company’s own tasks, data and controls.

▸ Computer use gets stronger controls
OpenAI reports Astra produced unintended outcomes 89% less often than GPT-5.6 Sol on its internal computer-use safety benchmark.
Enterprise administrators can restrict approved sites, applications, uploads and downloads.
That makes staged deployment more realistic, but the evidence is still the vendor’s own.

▸ OpenAI asks for mandatory frontier rules
OpenAI now supports capability-based national safety requirements, independent assessments and serious-incident reporting.
It also endorsed four California bills covering assessors, auditors, young users and biological safeguards.
For business buyers, regulation is moving from broad principles toward evidence, monitoring and disclosure.

▸ The AI detector that would not behave
One builder spent more than ten hours trying to make a reliable AI-video detector.
Gemini repeatedly disagreed with a specialist detection service, and six test videos consumed more than 12,000 service operations.
The prototype eventually worked better, but was too unpredictable in cost to offer publicly.
Cheap building did not produce cheap, trustworthy operations.

▸ Today’s models can disrupt work for years
Ethan Mollick argued that even a complete halt in model progress would leave at least a decade of change.
The reason is diffusion: better workflows and wider use keep extracting value from capabilities that already exist.
Companies waiting for the model market to settle are waiting for the wrong thing.
