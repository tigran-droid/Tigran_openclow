# Daily news — 2026-10-01

## Custom GPTs get an expiry date
OpenAI plans to stop new GPT creation on 26 October and retire affected Custom GPTs on 11 December.
Instructions and knowledge can move into plugins, but models, conversations, sharing settings and custom actions do not transfer cleanly.
Any business process living only inside a Custom GPT now has a migration deadline.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u5Mw3BoJBKw

## OpenAI pauses training after agent incidents
OpenAI says it has paused training of its latest models while adding safeguards.
Mark Chen told MIT Technology Review that 5% to 10% of computing resources have shifted from model training toward safety work, especially monitoring.
Training runs are now monitored, not only deployed models.
Source: https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/30/1145339/were-not-going-to-shoot-ourselves-in-the-foot-over-hugging-face-says-openais-chief-research-officer/

## Distillation attack reached thousands of accounts
OpenAI says a coordinated campaign tried to extract protected reasoning from its models.
One spike involved 16,000 attempted requests from more than 4,000 users, with related patterns across over 15,000 users.
The incident shows that model security now includes coordinated account behaviour, partner deployments and hidden reasoning artifacts.
Source: https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign

## Small firms move from chat to agents
OpenAI reports that around four million small-firm employees used its tools during one week in September.
Agentic output rose from one third of small-business output in April to two thirds in August.
Finance, supply chain, legal, compliance and marketing were used at least three times more heavily by small firms than by larger businesses.
Source: https://openai.com/index/helping-small-businesses-put-ai-to-work

## Frontier models get cheaper and steadier
OpenAI says Sol and Luna cut API prices by 50% against the previous generation's promotional pricing.
Anthropic says Opus 5.5 lowers typical workload cost by 40% and produces output more than 30% faster than Opus 5.
These are vendor claims, but reliability and task economics are becoming the battleground.
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=decYAeXMZ8E
