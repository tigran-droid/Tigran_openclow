# Daily news — 2026-08-26

▸ Gates says the thresholds are behind us
Bill Gates says AI has crossed meaningful thresholds in cyber, biology, psychosocial dependence, employment and possible loss of control.
He argues that any model capable of designing novel molecules should be monitored.
His proposed responses include human-reserved jobs and taxes on tokens or robots to fund transition costs.
For leaders, speculative risk is becoming an operating-policy question.

▸ Safety now consumes real capacity
OpenAI paused some advanced reinforcement-learning work after cyber concerns.
Its stated target is to alert on concerning activity within 30 minutes.
The monitoring is estimated to consume roughly 20% of the compute used by the models it watches.
Safety is no longer a paragraph in a policy. It is becoming a material operating cost.

▸ Agent adoption remains mostly experimental
In a poll of an AI-forward audience, 18% said they were deploying agents aggressively and 49% were selectively piloting.
One host estimated aggressive deployment across the wider US business market at under 3% based on executive conversations.
The gap is not access to models. It is the ability to define work, evidence and ownership.

▸ China owns the humanoid supply line
MIT Technology Review reports that nearly 90% of more than 13,000 two-armed, two-legged robots delivered globally last year were made in China.
Public demonstrations are normalising robots while manufacturers work through safety, cost and battery constraints.
Embodied AI is becoming an industrial capability race, not just a demonstration category.

▸ AI products are ageing in public
Ethan Mollick noted that OpenAI's October 2025 vision for enterprise agents already looks dated and that Agent Builder was killed in June.
Yet enterprise products still use that architecture.
Buyers need portable workflows, data and acceptance tests because interfaces will depreciate faster than business processes.
